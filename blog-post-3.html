Commission-based pilots: a practical model for new market development

August 2026 · Gulf Atlantic Zone

Exclusivity and inventory commitments are often discussed too early. For suppliers entering Ghana—and for local partners building the pipeline—a commission-based, non-exclusive pilot is usually the more professional starting point.

What a good pilot agreement covers

  • Scope: products, geography, and duration (e.g. three months)
  • Lead registration: how leads are submitted, acknowledged, and tracked
  • Commission: rate, calculation basis, eligible sales, exclusions, payment timing
  • Treatment of existing accounts, pre-existing leads, and multi-party introductions
  • Reporting: pipeline updates and end-of-pilot review

Importantly, the local partner should not quote binding prices, promise stock, or commit the supplier to delivery or warranty terms without written confirmation. That discipline protects both parties and builds trust with buyers.

From pilot to partnership

At the end of the pilot period, both sides review qualified leads, quotation activity, conversions, pricing feedback, and after-sales readiness. Based on evidence, they can continue, adjust the product mix, set targets, explore dealer relationships, or discuss broader representation.

This model aligns incentives: the local partner earns when value is created; the supplier only scales commitment when demand is real. It is not the only model—but for agricultural technology entering Ghana, it is often the most responsible place to start.
